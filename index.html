Experiment 01 Single Qubit

Qubit and the Bloch Sphere

Aim: See how H and X gates move a qubit, and what measuring does to it.
Play Voice-Over Narration English Studio Narration
0:00 / 0:00
Interactive 3D Bloch Sphere Drag with mouse to rotate perspective
Experimental Procedure Click step to mark complete
  • Start with the qubit in eigenstate |0⟩ at the north pole.
  • Press Apply H and observe the vector transition to the equator.
  • Press Apply X and watch it flip to the opposite hemisphere.
  • Press Measure and note which pole the state vector collapses to.
  • Press Reset to |0⟩ and repeat measurement trials.
Scientific Theory

A pure qubit state is geometrically represented by a unit vector on the surface of the Bloch sphere: |0⟩ at the north pole (z = +1) and |1⟩ at the south pole (z = -1). The Hadamard gate H maps |0⟩ to the equal superposition (|+⟩ = (|0⟩ + |1⟩)/√2) located on the equator. Pauli-X acts as a π-rotation around the x-axis (quantum NOT). Projective measurement forces wave-function collapse to an eigenstate with probabilities given by the Born rule P(i) = |⟨i|ψ⟩|².

Laboratory Observation

Observation: H moves the arrow from the north pole to the equator, an equal mix of zero and one. X flips it to the south pole. Measuring snaps the arrow to one pole.

Viva Voce Examination Questions
1. What does the north pole of the Bloch sphere represent?
The north pole corresponds to the computational basis state |0⟩ with spherical polar coordinates θ = 0.
2. What does the Hadamard (H) gate do to |0⟩?
It creates an equal superposition state |+⟩ = (|0⟩ + |1⟩)/√2, positioning the state vector precisely on the equator along the +x axis.
3. What physical phenomenon occurs during measurement?
Wavefunction collapse: the coherent quantum superposition collapses irreversibly to one of the measurement operator's eigenstates (|0⟩ or |1⟩).
4. Why does Pauli-X invert the state?
Pauli-X corresponds to a 180° (π radian) rotation around the x-axis of the Bloch sphere, swapping amplitudes of |0⟩ and |1⟩.

Scientific Theory

In quantum computing, a single measurement yields a nondeterministic binary outcome governed by the Born probability rule. Repeated execution over N independent circuit runs (called "shots") reveals the underlying probability distribution. According to the law of large numbers, the statistical error decreases as 1/√N. Applying two consecutive Hadamard operations yields H² = I, exhibiting constructive interference for |0⟩ and complete destructive cancellation for |1⟩.

Laboratory Observation

Observation: with H the result is a fair coin. It is close to fifty fifty, and gets closer with more shots. Applying H twice returns the qubit to zero, which is interference.

Viva Voce Examination Questions
1. Why do 10 shots rarely yield an exact 5:5 split for H|0⟩?
Each shot is an independent Bernoulli trial. Low sample sizes have substantial shot noise / Poisson variance (σ = √[Np(1-p)]). Only asymptotic large N approaches the true expectation value.
2. What is the definition of a quantum "shot"?
A single complete execution cycle of circuit initialization, gate operations, and projective readout.
3. Why does H applied twice always return |0⟩?
H is its own Hermitian inverse (H = H†, H² = I). The amplitudes along the two alternative quantum paths to |1⟩ interfere destructively (+1/2 and -1/2 = 0).

Scientific Theory

The Hadamard gate places qubit 0 into superposition (|0⟩ + |1⟩)/√2. The subsequent CNOT gate conditionally flips qubit 1 if qubit 0 is |1⟩. This maps product state |+0⟩ to non-separable Bell state |Φ⁺⟩ = (|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2. The state cannot be factored into independent single-qubit states |ψ₁⟩ ⊗ |ψ₂⟩. Measuring one qubit instantaneously collapses both qubits into the same value.

Laboratory Observation

Observation: H on the first qubit gives an equal mix of zero zero and one zero. CNOT then links the second qubit. Measurements give only zero zero or one one, each about half, and never zero one or one zero.

Viva Voce Examination Questions
1. Which gates synthesize the canonical Bell state |Φ⁺⟩?
A Hadamard gate on the control qubit followed by a CNOT gate acting between the control and target qubits.
2. Why do outcomes |01⟩ and |10⟩ never appear?
The statevector amplitude for |01⟩ and |10⟩ is strictly zero. By the Born rule, P = |0|² = 0.
3. Does entanglement permit faster-than-light communication?
No (No-Communication Theorem). While correlations are instantaneous, individual measurements are completely random, so no information can be transmitted without a classical channel.

Scientific Theory

CNOT (Controlled-NOT or Feynman gate) is a fundamental two-qubit unitary gate with matrix representation diag(I, X). It transforms basis states according to |c, t⟩ → |c, c ⊕ t⟩ where ⊕ denotes modulo-2 addition (XOR). Because quantum mechanics is unitary, all quantum gates must be reversible; CNOT is its own inverse (CNOT² = I).

Laboratory Observation

Observation: CNOT flips the target only when the control is one. So zero one stays, one zero becomes one one, and one one becomes one zero.

Viva Voce Examination Questions
1. Under what condition does CNOT invert the target qubit?
Strictly when the control qubit is in computational basis state |1⟩.
2. Is CNOT a universal quantum gate on its own?
No, but CNOT combined with arbitrary single-qubit rotations forms a universal quantum gate set.
Experiment 05 Interference

Quantum Interference with Phase Tuning

Aim: Investigate how a relative phase shift governs interference fringes in a Mach-Zehnder style circuit (H → R_z(ϕ) → H).
Play Voice-Over NarrationEnglish Studio Narration
0:00 / 0:00
Interference Fringe & Transition Wave
Experimental Procedure
  • Set phase ϕ = 0° and execute 1000 shots. Verify outcome |0⟩ is 100%.
  • Adjust phase slider to 90°: observe equal 50:50 probabilities.
  • Slide phase to 180°: verify complete reversal to 100% |1⟩ outcome.
  • Observe continuous modulation of probability P(|1⟩) = sin²(ϕ/2).
Scientific Theory

The sequence H → R_z(ϕ) → H serves as the quantum computational analogue of an optical Mach-Zehnder interferometer. The initial Hadamard acts as a 50:50 beam splitter creating two parallel paths. The phase gate introduces a relative phase difference e^(iϕ). The second Hadamard recombines the paths, converting quantum phase into observable amplitude interference.

Laboratory Observation

Observation: the chance of measuring 1 rises smoothly from 0 to 1 as the phase goes from 0° to 180°. This is quantum interference.

Viva Voce Examination Questions
1. Why is the outcome deterministically |0⟩ at phase ϕ = 0°?
At ϕ = 0°, R_z(0) = I. Therefore H • I • H = H² = I. The paths to state |1⟩ interfere destructively.
2. At what phase is output |1⟩ guaranteed?
At ϕ = 180° (π radians), where R_z(π) = Z. HZH = X (quantum NOT), mapping |0⟩ to |1⟩ with probability 1.
Experiment 06 Quantum Protocol

Quantum Teleportation Protocol

Aim: Transmit an arbitrary unknown quantum state |ψ⟩ from Alice to Bob using shared entanglement and two classical bits.
Play Voice-Over NarrationEnglish Studio Narration
0:00 / 0:00
Protocol State Engine
Press Advance Next Step to begin the protocol.
    Experimental Procedure
    • Choose the state angle θ to prepare an arbitrary state |ψ⟩ on q0.
    • Advance to distribute Bell pair |Φ⁺⟩ between Alice (q1) and Bob (q2).
    • Alice performs Bell basis measurement: CNOT (q0 → q1) followed by H(q0).
    • Alice sends two classical bits (m0, m1) to Bob.
    • Bob applies unitary correction X^(m1) Z^(m0) to reconstruct |ψ⟩ with 100% fidelity.
    Scientific Theory

    Quantum teleportation transfers the quantum information of an unknown state |ψ⟩ using 1 ebit of entanglement and 2 classical bits without violating the No-Cloning theorem. Alice's projective measurement destroys her original state. Bob uses the classical measurement bits to apply one of the four Pauli operations {I, X, Z, XZ}, perfectly reconstructing |ψ⟩ on his qubit.

    Laboratory Observation

    Observation: after the correction, Bob’s qubit matches the original with fidelity 100%, whatever the measurement result was.

    Viva Voce Examination Questions
    1. Does teleportation transport matter or energy across space?
    No. Only quantum state information is reconstructed. The physical qubit carrier remains locally at Bob's station.
    2. Why does teleportation not violate the No-Cloning Theorem?
    Because Alice's projective measurement destroys the original quantum state at her node before Bob reconstructs it. Only one copy ever exists.

    Experimental Procedure
    • Set Bob's angle θ_B = 45° and press Run 1000 Pair Shots.
    • Inspect the four correlation terms: E(a,b), E(a,b'), E(a',b), and E(a',b').
    • Confirm that CHSH parameter S ≈ 2.83, exceeding the classical bound of 2.0.

    Scientific Theory

    The Clauser-Horne-Shimony-Holt (CHSH) inequality bounds correlations in any physical theory based on Local Realism (Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen hidden variables): |S| ≤ 2. Quantum mechanics predicts S = 2√2 ≈ 2.828 (Tsirelson's bound) when measurement angles are separated by 45°. Experimental violation of S ≤ 2 confirms nature is intrinsically non-local.

    Laboratory Observation

    Observation: at 45° the score is about 2.8, above the classical limit of 2, which shows the correlations are truly quantum.

    Viva Voce Examination Questions
    1. What is the classical upper limit of the CHSH parameter S?
    Under local realism, |S| ≤ 2.
    2. What is Tsirelson's bound?
    The maximum quantum mechanical violation permitted by Hilbert space geometry: S = 2√2 ≈ 2.8284.
    Experiment 08 Quantum Hardware

    Decoherence & Environmental Noise Simulation

    Aim: Examine how environmental noise and bit-flip errors degrade Bell state entanglement fidelity.
    Play Voice-Over NarrationEnglish Studio Narration
    0:00 / 0:00
    Depolarizing Channel Noise Engine
    Experimental Procedure
    • Set error rate to 0% and execute 1000 shots. Confirm only |00⟩ and |11⟩ appear.
    • Increase noise rate to 10% and sample again. Observe erroneous |01⟩ and |10⟩ states.
    • Increase noise rate to 30%: note how Bell pair fidelity collapses towards a mixed state.
    Scientific Theory

    Real-world quantum processors are open quantum systems subject to thermal fluctuations and stray electromagnetic coupling. Unwanted interactions cause relaxation (T1) and dephasing (T2). Under an independent bit-flip channel with error probability p, erroneous orthogonal states |01⟩ and |10⟩ are populated, motivating the necessity of Quantum Error Correction (QEC) codes.

    Laboratory Observation

    Observation: as the error rate rises, the wrong pairs 01 and 10 grow and the share of correct pairs falls. This is why error correction is needed.

    Viva Voce Examination Questions
    1. What is quantum decoherence?
    The loss of quantum coherence and phase information due to uncontrolled entanglement between the quantum system and its thermal environment.
    2. How do quantum computers counteract environmental noise?
    By employing topological surface codes and fault-tolerant quantum error correction, encoding a single logical qubit across multiple physical qubits via stabilizer syndrome measurements.
    Experiment 09 Quantum Speedup

    Grover's Quantum Search Algorithm

    Aim: Locate a marked item in an unstructured 4-element database in a single query using Grover amplitude amplification.
    Play Voice-Over NarrationEnglish Studio Narration
    0:00 / 0:00
    Amplitude Amplification & Inversion-About-Mean
    Experimental Procedure
    • Select target item |10⟩ and press Next Algorithm Step.
    • Watch Hadamard transform initialize equal superposition (+0.50 across all 4 items).
    • Apply Oracle: observe the marked item amplitude flip sign to -0.50.
    • Apply Diffusion: amplitudes reflect about the mean, boosting target to 1.00.
    • Execute Measurement: target item is retrieved with 100% deterministic success!
    Scientific Theory

    Grover's algorithm searches an unsorted database of N items in O(√N) oracle queries compared to O(N) classically. For N = 4 (2 qubits), exact resonance is reached in a single query: the oracle inverts the phase of the target |ω⟩ (U_ω |x⟩ = (-1)^(f(x)) |x⟩), and the Grover diffusion operator 2|s⟩⟨s| - I reflects all amplitudes across the mean, boosting the target probability to 100%.

    Laboratory Observation

    Observation: after one oracle and one diffusion step, the marked item has probability 100%.

    Viva Voce Examination Questions
    1. How many queries does Grover's search need for an N=4 database?
    Exactly 1 query, achieving 100% success probability. A classical linear search requires up to 3 queries.
    2. What is the mathematical function of the Grover diffusion operator?
    Inversion about the mean: for each amplitude a_i, it computes a'_i = 2⟨a⟩ - a_i, boosting amplitudes that are below average after the oracle phase flip.

    • Select Entangled Bell Pair and sample 200 pairs. Note the 100% correlation match rate.
    • Switch to Independent Qubits (H on each) and run 200 pairs.
    • Observe that independent qubits agree only ~50% of the time by chance.
    Scientific Theory

    For independent qubits in state |++⟩ = 1/2 (|00⟩ + |01⟩ + |10⟩ + |11⟩), measurement outcomes are completely uncorrelated; matching results occur with 50% probability purely due to random chance. In contrast, an entangled Bell pair shares a single non-local wave function where outcomes correlate with 100% certainty, demonstrating the distinction between product states and entangled quantum states.

    Laboratory Observation

    Observation: the entangled pair matches every time, 100%. The independent pair matches about half the time.

    Viva Voce Examination Questions
    1. Why do independent qubits agree ~50% of the time?
    Each qubit has independent 50% probability for 0 or 1. P(match) = P(00) + P(11) = 0.25 + 0.25 = 0.50 (classical binomial coincidence).
    2. Can quantum entanglement be cloned?
    No. By the No-Cloning theorem and Monogamy of Entanglement, an entangled state cannot be copied or jointly shared with an arbitrary third party.
